First-angle orthographic projection: The front view (FV) is projected onto VP and placed above the XY reference line. The top view (TV) is projected onto HP and placed below XY. XY represents the intersection of HP and VP. Perpendicular projectors align corresponding points between views. A point or edge lying in HP projects onto XY in the front view; a point or edge lying in VP projects onto XY in the top view. The 3D reference-plane projections and the 2D sheet are derived from the same transformed geometry.

DRAWING METHOD

Think in stages

  1. 01
    Establish the simple position

    Identify the solid, its base, axis, apex and defining dimensions. Draw the initial front view and top view in the simple position specified by the problem.

  2. 02
    Apply the inclination condition

    Incline the specified axis, edge or face to the required angle while maintaining the stated parallelism to the other reference plane. For applicable solids, set the apex direction and resting condition.

  3. 03
    Project corresponding points

    Project the final solid onto VP to obtain FV and onto HP to obtain TV. Use perpendicular projectors and keep corresponding points aligned according to first-angle projection.

  4. 04
    Verify the final views

    Check the specified inclination, contact with HP or VP, alignment with XY, true and foreshortened lengths, and visible or hidden edges.

Learning note: This interactive model illustrates selected solids, inclination conditions, applicable resting conditions and their corresponding front and top views. The result depends on the selected solid, angle, reference plane, apex direction and contact feature. Use the model to understand geometric relationships; for a formal engineering drawing, follow the problem-specific construction method and prescribed projection conventions. Verify exact hinge construction and hidden-edge visibility where required.
